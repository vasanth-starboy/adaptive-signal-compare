🚦 Adaptive signal control: 8-parameter comparison

Your algorithm versus Fixed-time and four published algorithms (Max-Pressure, MFAPC, MPC-Road, MPC-CasADi). The four come from one study that tested all of them on the same 6-intersection network with the same metrics, which keeps the comparison fair. Your own numbers come from your dataset, so the two sets are close in kind but not identical in conditions.

Step 1 · Load the dataset and generate the result files

Choose your raw probe CSV (columns: timestamp, probe_id, speed, approach_id, optional signal_id), edit the settings if you like, and press Run. Every graph on this page then fills in from the result. This is a simplified browser copy of the Python program: it skips the Wmin offset refinement and does not compute prediction error, demand accuracy or the scalability test, so use Python for final report numbers.

Scoreboard: where your algorithm is ahead or behind





1. Average delay: reduction vs fixed-time (%)


Yours and fixed-time are measured on your data. The other four are published values from one study, computed from its table. Higher is better.


2. Average travel time: reduction vs fixed-time (%)


Same sources as graph 1, using total network travel time. Higher is better.


3. Arrival-on-green ratio (%)


Both are measured on your data (adaptive offsets against offsets all 0). A value of 0 is a real result: fixed-time with all offsets 0 gives no arrivals on green. No published arrival-on-green values were found for the other algorithms.


4. Throughput (veh/h, whole network)


Served flow = demand the signal plan can actually serve (green-limited), averaged over windows, for your plan and for fixed-time. The probe-based estimate and the ground-truth count are network totals; ground truth needs a ground-truth file. Equal served flows mean no approach was over capacity.


5. Travel-time prediction error (MAPE %)


Both bars are measured on your data: the flowchart’s arithmetic-mean speed against a harmonic-mean variant. Lower is better.


6. Demand estimation accuracy (%)


Your algorithm only. It needs a ground-truth file and is measured against it.


7. Computation time per control update (ms, log scale)


Published times are whole-network updates for 6 intersections on the authors’ hardware. Yours depends on your computer. Lower is better.


8. Scalability: update time vs number of intersections (ms, log scale)


Your curve is from section 8(b) of your report. Published points exist only for 6 intersections.
